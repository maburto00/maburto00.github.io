## A case history in scientific method (Skinner 1956)
1. "When you run into something interesting, drop everything else and study it"
2. "Some ways of doing research are easier than others": **simplyfing complex experiments** may lead to new ideas
3. "Some people are lucky": **unpolished details** may lead to new ideas
4. "Apparatuses sometimes break down": **unexpected errors** may lead to new ideas
5. "**Serendipity**: the art of finding one thing while looking for something else": the thing you were trying to show may prove wrong/false in the end, but by pursuing that idea you may end up finding something interesting 

## Ultralearning (Scott H. Young)
1. Meta-Learning (first draw a map)
2. Focus (sharpen your knife)
3. **Directness** (go straight ahead)
4. Drill (attack your weakest point)
5. Retrieval (test to learn)
6. **Feedback** (don't dodge the punches)
7. Retention (don't fill a leaky bucket)
8. Intuition (dig deep before building up)
9. Experimentation (explore outside your comfort zone)

## Deep Work (Cal Newport)
1. Work deeply
    - **Depth philosophy**: monastic, bimodal, rhythmic, journalistic
    - **Ritualize**: where, how long, how you will work, how you will support your work
    - **Grand gestures**: 
    - **Don't work alone**:
    - **Execute like a business**:
        1. focus on the **Wildly Important**
        2. Act on the **Lead Measures**
        3. keep a compelling **Scoreboard**
        4. create a **Cadence of Accountability**
    - **Be lazy**
        1. Downtime aids *insights*
        2. Downtime helps recharge the *energy* needed to work deeply
        3. Evening work done instead of downtime is usually not that important
2. Embrace boredom
3. Quit social media
4. Drain the shallows

## Slow productivity (Cal Newport)
1. Do **Fewer Things**
2. Work at a **Natural Pace**
3. Obsess Over **Quality**

## Atomic habits (James Clear)
**Laws**
1. Make it obvious (**Cue**)
2. Make it attractive (**Desire**)
3. Make it easy (**Activity**)
4. Make it rewarding (**Reward**)
